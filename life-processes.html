1. Introduction to Life Processes

All living organisms perform certain basic activities to maintain life. These activities are called life processes.

The main life processes studied in Class 10 are:

  • Nutrition
  • Respiration
  • Transportation
  • Excretion
Remember: Life processes are necessary for obtaining energy, maintaining the body and removing waste materials.

2. Nutrition

Nutrition is the process by which organisms obtain food and use it for energy, growth and maintenance.

Types of Nutrition

Type Description Example
Autotrophic Organisms make their own food. Green plants
Heterotrophic Organisms obtain food from other organisms. Animals

3. Photosynthesis

Green plants prepare their food by the process of photosynthesis.

Carbon dioxide + Water → Glucose + Oxygen

Sunlight and chlorophyll are required for photosynthesis.

Raw Materials Required

  • Carbon dioxide
  • Water
  • Sunlight
  • Chlorophyll
Important: Chlorophyll traps solar energy and helps plants convert carbon dioxide and water into food.

4. Human Digestive System

Digestion is the process of breaking complex food substances into simpler substances that can be absorbed by the body.

Main Parts

  1. Mouth
  2. Oesophagus
  3. Stomach
  4. Small intestine
  5. Large intestine
  6. Rectum
  7. Anus

Important Digestive Glands

  • Salivary glands
  • Liver
  • Pancreas

5. Digestion in Different Parts

Mouth

Food is chewed and mixed with saliva. Salivary amylase begins the digestion of starch.

Stomach

The stomach releases hydrochloric acid and digestive juices. Protein digestion begins in the stomach.

Small Intestine

Most digestion and absorption of nutrients takes place in the small intestine.

Large Intestine

The large intestine mainly absorbs water and helps in the formation of faeces.

6. Respiration

Respiration is the process by which food is broken down to release energy.

Aerobic Respiration

Respiration that takes place in the presence of oxygen is called aerobic respiration.

Glucose + Oxygen → Carbon dioxide + Water + Energy

Anaerobic Respiration

Respiration that occurs without oxygen is called anaerobic respiration.

In yeast, anaerobic respiration produces alcohol, carbon dioxide and energy.
Aerobic Anaerobic
Requires oxygen Does not require oxygen
More energy is released Less energy is released

8. Transportation in Humans

The transportation system carries oxygen, nutrients, hormones and waste materials throughout the body.

Blood

  • Red blood cells — transport oxygen.
  • White blood cells — help defend the body.
  • Platelets — help in blood clotting.
  • Plasma — transports dissolved substances.

Blood Vessels

Blood Vessel Function
Arteries Carry blood away from the heart.
Veins Carry blood towards the heart.
Capillaries Allow exchange of materials with tissues.

9. Human Heart

The human heart is a muscular organ that pumps blood throughout the body.

Four Chambers

  • Right atrium
  • Right ventricle
  • Left atrium
  • Left ventricle

Double circulation: In humans, blood passes through the heart twice during one complete cycle of circulation.

10. Transportation in Plants

Xylem

Xylem transports water and minerals from the roots to other parts of the plant.

Phloem

Phloem transports food prepared by leaves to different parts of the plant.

Tissue Transport
Xylem Water and minerals
Phloem Food

11. Transpiration

The loss of water in the form of water vapour from the aerial parts of plants is called transpiration.

Importance

  • Creates transpiration pull.
  • Helps in upward movement of water.
  • Helps cool the plant.

12. Excretion

The process of removal of harmful metabolic waste products from the body is called excretion.

Human Excretory System

  • Kidneys
  • Ureters
  • Urinary bladder
  • Urethra
📌 Diagram Practice Area

Draw and label the Human Excretory System.

13. Nephron

The nephron is the structural and functional unit of the kidney.

Main Functions

  1. Filtration of blood.
  2. Reabsorption of useful substances.
  3. Removal of wastes in the form of urine.
Remember: Each kidney contains a large number of nephrons.

14. Excretion in Plants

Plants remove waste products in different ways.

  • Gases may be removed through stomata.
  • Excess water is removed by transpiration.
  • Some wastes are stored in leaves, bark and old tissues.
  • Some wastes are stored as resins and gums.

15. Important Differences

Process Main Function
Nutrition Obtaining and using food
Respiration Release of energy from food
Transportation Movement of substances
Excretion Removal of metabolic wastes

16. Solved Questions

Q1. What are life processes?

Answer: The basic processes necessary for maintaining life, such as nutrition, respiration, transportation and excretion, are called life processes.
Q2. What is photosynthesis?

Answer: Photosynthesis is the process by which green plants prepare food from carbon dioxide and water using sunlight and chlorophyll.
Q3. What is the function of xylem?

Answer: Xylem transports water and minerals from roots to other parts of the plant.
Q4. What is a nephron?

Answer: Nephron is the structural and functional unit of the kidney.

1. जीवन प्रक्रम का परिचय

सभी जीवित organisms जीवन को बनाए रखने के लिए कुछ मूलभूत क्रियाएँ करते हैं। इन्हें जीवन प्रक्रम कहते हैं।

  • पोषण
  • श्वसन
  • परिवहन
  • उत्सर्जन
जीवन प्रक्रम शरीर को ऊर्जा प्राप्त करने, वृद्धि करने, शरीर को बनाए रखने और अपशिष्ट पदार्थों को हटाने में सहायता करते हैं।

2. पोषण

पोषण वह प्रक्रिया है जिसके द्वारा जीव भोजन प्राप्त करते हैं और उसका उपयोग ऊर्जा, वृद्धि तथा शरीर के रखरखाव के लिए करते हैं।

प्रकार अर्थ उदाहरण
स्वपोषी जीव अपना भोजन स्वयं बनाते हैं। हरे पौधे
परपोषी जीव अन्य जीवों से भोजन प्राप्त करते हैं। मनुष्य/जानवर

3. प्रकाश संश्लेषण

हरे पौधे प्रकाश संश्लेषण द्वारा अपना भोजन बनाते हैं।

कार्बन डाइऑक्साइड + जल → ग्लूकोज़ + ऑक्सीजन

इस प्रक्रिया के लिए सूर्य का प्रकाश और क्लोरोफिल आवश्यक हैं।

  • कार्बन डाइऑक्साइड
  • जल
  • सूर्य का प्रकाश
  • क्लोरोफिल

7. मनुष्य में परिवहन

परिवहन तंत्र शरीर में ऑक्सीजन, पोषक पदार्थ, हार्मोन और अपशिष्ट पदार्थों के आवागमन में सहायता करता है।

रक्त

  • लाल रक्त कोशिकाएँ — ऑक्सीजन का परिवहन।
  • श्वेत रक्त कोशिकाएँ — शरीर की रक्षा।
  • प्लेटलेट्स — रक्त का थक्का बनाने में सहायता।
  • प्लाज्मा — विभिन्न घुले पदार्थों का परिवहन।

रक्त वाहिकाएँ

केशिकाएँ ऊतकों के साथ पदार्थों के आदान-प्रदान में सहायता करती हैं।

12. नेफ्रॉन

नेफ्रॉन वृक्क की संरचनात्मक और क्रियात्मक इकाई है।

  1. रक्त का निस्यंदन
  2. उपयोगी पदार्थों का पुनःअवशोषण
  3. मूत्र के रूप में अपशिष्ट पदार्थों का निष्कासन
याद रखें: प्रत्येक वृक्क में बहुत बड़ी संख्या में नेफ्रॉन पाए जाते हैं।
