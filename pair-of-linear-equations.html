Introduction

A pair of linear equations in two variables contains two linear equations with two variables. In this chapter we learn different methods to solve them and understand their graphs.

1. General Form

The general form of a pair of linear equations in two variables is:

a₁x + b₁y + c₁ = 0
a₂x + b₂y + c₂ = 0

Here a₁, b₁, c₁, a₂, b₂ and c₂ are real numbers.

2. Graphical Method

Each linear equation represents a straight line on the Cartesian plane. The solution of the pair is the point where the two lines intersect.

Possible Cases

  • Intersecting lines → One solution
  • Parallel lines → No solution
  • Coincident lines → Infinitely many solutions

3. Conditions for Number of Solutions

For the equations:

a₁x + b₁y + c₁ = 0
a₂x + b₂y + c₂ = 0

One Solution

a₁/a₂ ≠ b₁/b₂

The two lines intersect at one point.

No Solution

a₁/a₂ = b₁/b₂ ≠ c₁/c₂

The two lines are parallel.

Infinitely Many Solutions

a₁/a₂ = b₁/b₂ = c₁/c₂

The two lines are coincident.

4. Substitution Method

In this method, we find one variable in terms of the other and substitute it into the second equation.

Solved Example:

Solve:
x + y = 7
x − y = 1

From x + y = 7
x = 7 − y

Substitute in x − y = 1:
7 − y − y = 1
7 − 2y = 1
−2y = −6
y = 3

x = 7 − 3 = 4

Answer: x = 4, y = 3

5. Elimination Method

In this method, we eliminate one variable by adding, subtracting or multiplying the equations suitably.

Solved Example:

Solve:
2x + y = 7
x + y = 5

Subtract the second equation from the first:

(2x + y) − (x + y) = 7 − 5
x = 2

Put x = 2 in x + y = 5:
2 + y = 5
y = 3

Answer: x = 2, y = 3

6. Cross-Multiplication Method

For equations:

a₁x + b₁y + c₁ = 0
a₂x + b₂y + c₂ = 0

The cross-multiplication formulas are:

x/(b₁c₂ − b₂c₁) = y/(c₁a₂ − c₂a₁) = 1/(a₁b₂ − a₂b₁)

7. Word Problems

Many real-life problems can be converted into a pair of linear equations and solved using algebraic methods.

Solved Example:

The sum of two numbers is 10 and their difference is 2. Find the numbers.

Let the numbers be x and y.

x + y = 10
x − y = 2

Adding:
2x = 12
x = 6

6 + y = 10
y = 4

Answer: The two numbers are 6 and 4.

Important Points for Exam

  • Learn all four methods carefully.
  • Remember the conditions for one, no and infinitely many solutions.
  • Always check the final values in both equations.
  • Read word problems carefully before forming equations.
  • Write each step clearly in the exam.

Practice Questions

  1. Solve: x + y = 8 and x − y = 2.
  2. Solve: 2x + y = 7 and x + y = 5.
  3. Find the number of solutions of: 2x + 3y = 5 and 4x + 6y = 10.
  4. Solve using substitution method: 3x + y = 11 and x − y = 1.
  5. The sum of two numbers is 15 and their difference is 3. Find the numbers.
