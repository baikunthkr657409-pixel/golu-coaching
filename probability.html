1. Introduction

Probability is a branch of mathematics that deals with the chance or likelihood of an event happening.

Probability tells us how likely an event is to occur.

Examples of random experiments include tossing a coin, rolling a die, or drawing a card from a deck.

2. Random Experiment

An experiment in which the exact outcome cannot be predicted in advance is called a random experiment.

Examples

  • Tossing a coin.
  • Rolling a die.
  • Drawing a card from a well-shuffled deck.

3. Outcome

An outcome is a possible result of a random experiment.

Example:

When a coin is tossed, the possible outcomes are:

S = {H, T}

H = Head
T = Tail

4. Sample Space

The set of all possible outcomes of a random experiment is called the sample space.

Sample Space = Set of all possible outcomes
Example:

When a die is thrown:

S = {1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6}

Therefore:
Number of outcomes = 6

5. Event

An event is a collection of one or more outcomes of a random experiment.

Example:

When a die is thrown, getting an even number is an event.

E = {2, 4, 6}

6. Theoretical Probability

If all outcomes of an experiment are equally likely, the theoretical probability of an event E is:

P(E) = Number of favourable outcomes / Total number of outcomes

P(E) = n(E) / n(S)

Where:

  • n(E) = number of favourable outcomes
  • n(S) = total number of outcomes in the sample space

7. Probability of a Coin Toss

When a fair coin is tossed once:

S = {H, T}

P(H) = 1/2
P(T) = 1/2
Example:

What is the probability of getting a head?

Favourable outcomes = 1
Total outcomes = 2

P(H) = 1/2
= 0.5

8. Probability of Throwing a Die

When a fair die is thrown once:

S = {1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6}

n(S) = 6
Example 1:

Find the probability of getting an even number.

Even outcomes = {2,4,6}
Favourable outcomes = 3
Total outcomes = 6

P(E) = 3/6
= 1/2
Example 2:

Find the probability of getting a number greater than 4.

Favourable outcomes = {5,6}
P(E) = 2/6
= 1/3

9. Impossible Event

An event that cannot happen is called an impossible event.

Probability of an impossible event = 0

P(E) = 0
Example:

Getting 7 when a normal die is thrown.

P(7) = 0

10. Sure Event

An event that must happen is called a sure event.

Probability of a sure event = 1

P(E) = 1
Example:

When a die is thrown, getting a number less than 7 is a sure event.

P(E) = 1

11. Range of Probability

0 ≤ P(E) ≤ 1

The probability of every event lies between 0 and 1.

Probability can also be written as a fraction, decimal or percentage.

For example:
1/2 = 0.5 = 50%

12. Complementary Event

If E is an event, then the event that E does not occur is called the complementary event of E.

It is denoted by E'.

P(E') = 1 − P(E)

Also:
P(E) + P(E') = 1
Example:

If P(E) = 3/5, find P(E').

P(E') = 1 − 3/5
= 2/5
= 2/5

13. Experimental Probability

Experimental probability is based on the actual results obtained after performing an experiment several times.

Experimental Probability = Number of times the event occurs / Total number of trials
Example:

A coin is tossed 50 times and heads appear 28 times.

Experimental probability of getting head:
P(H) = 28/50
= 14/25

14. Theoretical vs Experimental Probability

Theoretical Probability Experimental Probability
Based on equally likely possible outcomes. Based on actual experiment results.
Uses favourable outcomes and total outcomes. Uses observed frequency and total trials.
For a fair coin, P(H) = 1/2. Actual result may be different from 1/2.

15. Probability of Cards

A standard deck contains 52 cards.

Type Number

Red cards 26
Black cards 26
Hearts 13
Diamonds 13
Clubs 13
Spades 13
Aces 4
Kings 4
Queens 4
Jacks 4
Example:

Find the probability of drawing a red card from a well-shuffled deck.

Favourable cards = 26
Total cards = 52

P(Red) = 26/52
= 1/2

16. Probability of Two Coins

When two coins are tossed together:

S = {HH, HT, TH, TT}

Total outcomes = 4
Example:

Probability of getting two heads:

Favourable outcome = HH
P(HH) = 1/4
Example:

Probability of getting exactly one head:

Favourable outcomes = HT, TH
P(E) = 2/4
= 1/2

17. Probability of Three Coins

When three coins are tossed together, the sample space contains 8 equally likely outcomes.

S = {HHH, HHT, HTH, THH, HTT, THT, TTH, TTT}

n(S) = 8
Example:

Probability of getting three heads:

P(HHH) = 1/8

18. Solved Example – Die

Question:
A die is thrown once. Find the probability of getting a prime number.

Solution: Sample space:
S = {1,2,3,4,5,6}

Prime numbers = {2,3,5}

Favourable outcomes = 3
Total outcomes = 6

P(E) = 3/6
= 1/2

19. Solved Example – Cards

Question:
A card is drawn from a standard deck of 52 cards. Find the probability of getting an ace.

Solution: Number of aces = 4
Total cards = 52

P(Ace) = 4/52
= 1/13

20. Solved Example – Complement

Question:
If the probability of an event is 2/7, find the probability that the event does not occur.

Solution: P(E') = 1 − P(E)

= 1 − 2/7
= 5/7

22. Common Mistakes

  • Favourable outcomes और total outcomes को confuse न करें।
  • Probability हमेशा 0 और 1 के बीच होती है।
  • Sample space सभी possible outcomes का set है।
  • Complement में 1 − P(E) करना न भूलें।
  • Cards के प्रश्न में total cards 52 याद रखें।
  • Die के प्रश्न में total outcomes 6 होते हैं।
  • दो coins के लिए total outcomes 4 होते हैं।
  • तीन coins के लिए total outcomes 8 होते हैं।

1. परिचय

प्रायिकता गणित की वह शाखा है जिसमें किसी घटना के होने की संभावना का अध्ययन किया जाता है।

Probability बताती है कि कोई घटना होने की कितनी संभावना है।

सिक्का उछालना, पासा फेंकना और ताश का पत्ता निकालना random experiments के उदाहरण हैं।

2. यादृच्छिक प्रयोग (Random Experiment)

ऐसा प्रयोग जिसका exact result पहले से निश्चित रूप से नहीं बताया जा सकता, random experiment कहलाता है।

  • सिक्का उछालना।
  • पासा फेंकना।
  • ताश के पत्तों में से एक पत्ता निकालना।

3. Outcome

Random experiment के किसी संभावित परिणाम को outcome कहते हैं।

उदाहरण:

एक सिक्का उछालने पर:

S = {H, T}

H = Head
T = Tail

4. Sample Space

Random experiment के सभी possible outcomes के set को sample space कहते हैं।

Sample Space = सभी संभावित परिणामों का समूह
उदाहरण:

एक पासा फेंकने पर:

S = {1,2,3,4,5,6}

Total outcomes = 6

5. Event

एक या अधिक outcomes के समूह को event कहते हैं।

उदाहरण:

पासा फेंकने पर even number मिलना एक event है।

E = {2,4,6}

6. Theoretical Probability

जब सभी outcomes equally likely हों, तो event E की theoretical probability:

P(E) = अनुकूल परिणामों की संख्या / कुल परिणामों की संख्या

P(E) = n(E) / n(S)
  • n(E) = favourable outcomes
  • n(S) = total outcomes

7. सिक्का उछालने की Probability

एक fair coin को एक बार उछालने पर:

S = {H,T}

P(H) = 1/2
P(T) = 1/2
उदाहरण:

Head आने की probability:

P(H) = 1/2
= 0.5

8. पासा फेंकने की Probability

एक fair die को एक बार फेंकने पर:

S = {1,2,3,4,5,6}

n(S) = 6
उदाहरण 1:

Even number आने की probability:

Even outcomes = {2,4,6}
P(E) = 3/6
= 1/2
उदाहरण 2:

4 से बड़ी संख्या आने की probability:

Favourable outcomes = {5,6}
P(E) = 2/6
= 1/3

9. असंभव घटना

जो घटना कभी नहीं हो सकती, उसे impossible event कहते हैं।

P(E) = 0
उदाहरण:

Normal die पर 7 आना impossible event है।
P(7) = 0

10. निश्चित घटना

जो घटना निश्चित रूप से होगी, उसे sure event कहते हैं।

P(E) = 1
उदाहरण:

पासा फेंकने पर 7 से छोटी संख्या आना sure event है।
P(E) = 1

11. Probability की Range

0 ≤ P(E) ≤ 1

किसी भी event की probability 0 और 1 के बीच होती है।

उदाहरण:

1/2 = 0.5 = 50%

12. Complementary Event

यदि E कोई event है, तो E के नहीं होने वाली घटना को उसका complementary event कहते हैं।

इसे E' से दर्शाते हैं।

P(E') = 1 − P(E)

P(E) + P(E') = 1
उदाहरण:

यदि P(E) = 3/5 है, तो:

P(E') = 1 − 3/5
= 2/5

13. Experimental Probability

किसी experiment को कई बार करने पर प्राप्त वास्तविक results के आधार पर निकाली गई probability को experimental probability कहते हैं।

Experimental Probability = घटना जितनी बार हुई / कुल trials
उदाहरण:

एक coin को 50 बार उछाला गया और 28 बार Head आया।

P(H) = 28/50
= 14/25

14. Theoretical और Experimental Probability

Theoretical Probability Experimental Probability
Possible outcomes पर आधारित होती है। Actual experiment के results पर आधारित होती है।
Favourable और total outcomes का उपयोग होता है। Observed frequency और total trials का उपयोग होता है।
Fair coin के लिए P(H)=1/2 Actual experiment में result अलग हो सकता है।
